\paragraph{Methods for comparison.}
We compare CEL with the ten methods in Table~\ref{tab:comparison}. Random
selection and verbalized confidence spend one call; the others are given a
budget of $k$ calls per case. All of them replay the shared cache, so they
see the same draws as CEL. Each method returns its own answer and is scored
on the correctness of that answer, so a method's accuracy can differ from
another's at the same budget, and it is reported beside the ranking metrics.
Every fitted quantity is frozen on the development slice before any
evaluation case is scored, and is refit at each $k$. Throughout, $m$ is the
number of the $n$ draws that agree with the majority answer, and a failed
extraction counts as a draw that disagrees. In our main configuration,
verbalized confidence $c_i \in [0, 100]$ rides on every draw, so it costs
no call and is available to every method.

\emph{Reference points.} Random selection gives the chance level, with the
exact hypergeometric null as reference. Verbalized confidence is the
cheapest informative score: the answer and confidence of a single draw.

\emph{Agreement.} Self-consistency \citep{wang2022} takes the majority of
$k$ draws of one fixed phrasing and ranks by agreement, which isolates
allocation at identical spend. Because confidence is free in our setting,
we add a control that ranks by agreement and breaks ties by the draws' mean
verbalized confidence. A gain over plain self-consistency alone could come
from reading confidences, so only a gain over this control supports the
allocation argument of Section~\ref{sec:method:budget}.

\emph{Rationale diversity.} Semantic entropy \citep{kuhn2023} groups the
draws' rationales by underlying reason and ranks by the negative
Miller--Madow entropy of the groups. One judge call groups all the
rationales of a case, and the method reads the labels of the draws it uses.
It needs at least two draws, so it starts at $k = 3$.

\emph{Combined and confidence-weighted scores.} BSDetector
\citep{chen2024} is the closest prior combined score. We reconstruct it
from the cache as a fixed blend of agreement and a self-reflection
certainty, with the first draw's stated confidence $c_1$ standing in for
their self-reflection prompt. The weight $0.7$ is our choice and is not fitted.
Avg-Conf \citep{xiong2023} aggregates verbalized confidence over samples,
taking $\tilde Y$ to be the majority answer, under both of their black-box
sampling schemes: self-random and prompting. CISC \citep{taubenfeld2025}
replaces the majority with a vote weighted by $\mathrm{softmax}(c_i/T)$,
with $T$ chosen for vote accuracy over their $80$-point grid on
$[10^{-4}, 10^{4}]$. CISC defines an answer, not a selection score, so we
rank by the winning answer's weight share, scaled down for failed
extractions. This score is our adaptation. It starts at $k = 2$.

\emph{Learned calibrator.} In the manner of \citet{pedapati2024}, we compute
two features per perturbation: the number of distinct answers and the mean
pairwise ROUGE-L of the full responses. A logistic regression of correctness
on the standardized features is fitted on the development slice. Below
$k = 5$ the only perturbation is stochastic decoding. From $k = 5$ the
method adds paraphrasing, with $\lceil (k-1)/2 \rceil$ draws of the original
wording and $\lfloor (k-1)/2 \rfloor$ paraphrase draws. Its answer is the
majority over all of them.

\paragraph{Departures from the source papers.}
Each follows from the yes/no format or the shared cache. On a yes/no answer
the number of distinct answers is $1$ or $2$, and ROUGE over answers alone is
a function of $m$, so we compute it over full responses to keep the
explanation's wording in play. We call the result a black-box calibrator,
not \citeauthor{pedapati2024}'s method, because it omits the perturbations
that need new prompts or a local NLI model: sentence permutation,
entity-frequency amplification, stop-word removal and split-response
consistency. We also omit \citeauthor{xiong2023}'s misleading-hint sampling
and Top-$K$ and Pair-Rank aggregation, of which Pair-Rank reduces to the
top-$2$ probabilities on a binary question, and CISC's P(True) confidence,
since all need new prompts. CISC with the answer token's probability in
place of the length-normalized response probability is a gray-box variant,
reported separately from the black-box comparisons.
